What is ASPI and S&P SL20?

When people say “the market was up today” on the CSE, they often mean a headline index moved. Two names you will see frequently are ASPI and S&P SL20.

Not investment advice. Indices describe groups of stocks; they are not recommendations to buy or sell any security.

ASPI (All Share Price Index)

ASPI is a broad indicator of price movement across listed companies on the CSE. When ASPI rises, the capitalisation-weighted picture of the market (as defined by the index rules) is higher than before; when it falls, that broad picture is lower. It is useful context: “Is my portfolio moving with the general market or very differently?”

S&P SL20

S&P SL20 focuses on a smaller set of larger, more liquid names (the exact constituents follow the published methodology). It often moves differently from ASPI when mid- and small-cap stocks behave unlike the largest counters.

How investors use indices (without over-reading them)

  • Context — a quiet day on your stocks may still sit inside a strong or weak market session.
  • Comparison — over long periods, people compare portfolio returns to an index; that is a benchmark habit, not a guarantee.
  • Not a crystal ball — yesterday’s index level does not tell you tomorrow’s prices.

Index up, my shares down

That can happen. Your holdings may be a small slice of the market, in different sectors, or affected by company-specific news. Indices average many names; your portfolio is yours alone.
