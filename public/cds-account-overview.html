CDS account overview for beginners

To hold listed shares in dematerialised (electronic) form on the Colombo market, investors use the Central Depository System (CDS) through a participant such as a stockbroker. This page is a plain overview, not an application guide.

Not investment advice. Account opening rules, documents and fees are set by CDS participants and regulators. Follow their official instructions.

What a CDS account is for

Paper share certificates are not how modern listed trading works day to day. A CDS account records your holdings electronically so buys and sells can settle through the market’s systems. Your broker relationship usually sits on top of that infrastructure.

Broker vs CDS (simple mental model)

  • Broker — places orders, provides research/tools, sends contract notes, may provide an app.
  • CDS account — the depository record of securities balances linked to you via that participant.

In practice you open a trading relationship with a licensed broker and complete the CDS-related account steps they require (identity documents, forms, bank details for payments).

Why portfolio apps still help

Broker statements are official. A personal tracker like CSE Portfolio does not replace them. It helps you organise history, costs and dividends in one place — especially if you ever change brokers or want offline backups of your notes.

Before you open anything

  • Use only licensed brokers and official channels.
  • Read fee schedules and account terms carefully.
  • Never share passwords, OTPs or recovery codes for trading or email accounts.
