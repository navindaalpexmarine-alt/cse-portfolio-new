How to calculate break-even sell price

Break-even sell price is the market price at which selling leaves you roughly even after buy costs already paid and sell costs still to pay. It is higher than your purchase price when costs are positive.

Not investment advice. Illustrative maths only. Use your real contract notes and broker rates.

Idea in one sentence

You need the net cash from the sale to at least match the total cash you spent to buy (for the shares you are selling).

Simple percentage model

Assume the same percentage cost rate c on buy and on sell (e.g. 0.0112 for 1.12%).

  • Buy cash out per share ≈ buy price × (1 + c)
  • Sell cash in per share ≈ sell price × (1 − c)
  • Set them equal: sell price × (1 − c) = buy price × (1 + c)
  • Therefore sell price ≈ buy price × (1 + c) / (1 − c)

With c = 1.12% (0.0112): factor ≈ 1.0112 / 0.9888 ≈ 1.0227. So break-even is roughly 2.27% above the buy price in this symmetric model — not merely 1.12% above.

Worked example

You bought at LKR 100.00 with 1.12% total buy costs.

  • True cost per share ≈ 100 × 1.0112 = LKR 101.12
  • Break-even sell price ≈ 100 × 1.0227 ≈ LKR 102.27
  • At LKR 102.27, sell net ≈ 102.27 × 0.9888 ≈ LKR 101.12

If the last trade is LKR 101.50 you might feel “up 1.5%” on the quote board while still below break-even after costs.

When the formula changes

  • Different buy vs sell rates → use each rate in the cash-out and cash-in legs.
  • Fixed fees → add them into total cash out / subtract from total cash in, then divide by quantity.
  • Partial sells → apply the same logic to the quantity you sell; keep average cost for the rest.
  • Dividends → optional: reduce effective cost by dividends received if you want an all-in view (be consistent).
