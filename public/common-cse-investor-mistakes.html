Common mistakes new CSE investors make

New investors often focus only on “which stock goes up.” Process mistakes — records, costs, concentration and emotion — quietly damage results even when a pick was reasonable.

Not investment advice. These are educational observations about habits, not stock picks.

1. Ignoring trading costs

Celebrating a 1% price rise after paying roughly that much (or more) round-trip in costs is a paper illusion. Learn your broker’s effective rates and use a break-even view before calling a trade a win. See commission explained and break-even sell price.

2. No written trade log

Memory mixes tips, prices and dates. Without a log you cannot compute average cost or review what actually worked. Record every buy and sell while the contract note is available.

3. Forgetting dividends

Income stocks look worse than they are if you only watch the price chart. Log dividends when cash arrives so total return is visible.

4. All-in on one story

Putting nearly all capital into a single counter or theme magnifies company-specific risk. Diversification does not remove risk, but extreme concentration is a common beginner pattern after one successful tip.

5. Chasing tips without a process

Social media and group chats move faster than verification. A minimum process: official filings, understanding how the company makes money, and a position size you can tolerate if you are wrong.

6. No backup of personal records

Browser data and phones get reset. Export or sync portfolio records so your history survives device changes.

A calmer alternative

Treat investing as a series of documented decisions. Tools help with arithmetic; discipline helps with behaviour. CSE Portfolio supports the arithmetic side; the behaviour side is yours.
