CSE market hours and settlement basics

Knowing when the market accepts orders and when trades settle helps you avoid confusion about cash, share availability and “why is my balance not updated yet?”

Not investment advice. Official session times and settlement cycles can change. Confirm on the CSE and with your broker.

Trading sessions

The CSE publishes regular trading session times for normal business days (with holidays and special sessions announced separately). Orders are handled inside those windows; outside them you generally cannot get a normal continuous-market execution.

Practical habit: check the calendar for public holidays and early closes before planning a same-day trade that must complete before a deadline (for example funding or travel).

What “settlement” means

Executing a trade (matching a buy and sell) is not the same moment as final exchange of cash and securities. Markets use a settlement cycle (often described as T+n, meaning n business days after trade date). Until settlement completes, your broker’s available cash or share balance may reflect rules that differ from the live last-traded price screen.

Why your app balance can lag

  • Trade date vs value date
  • Broker internal processing cut-offs
  • Bank transfer times for deposits and withdrawals

Contract notes and broker ledgers remain the reference for disputes; personal trackers record your intended history for analysis.

Portfolio tracking tip

Log trades on trade date with the price and costs from the contract note. If settlement timing matters for cash planning, keep a separate note of when funds are withdrawable according to your broker — do not assume instant access.
