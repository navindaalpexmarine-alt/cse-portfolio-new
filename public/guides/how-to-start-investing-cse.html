How to start investing on the Colombo Stock Exchange: a step-by-step guide

Investing on the Colombo Stock Exchange (CSE) used to mean visiting a broker's office with a stack of forms. Today most people can open an account online in a day or two and start trading from a phone. This guide walks through the steps and the decisions to make before your first trade.

Not investment advice. This guide explains the process. It does not suggest which shares to buy.

Step 0: decide whether you are ready

  • Do you have an emergency fund outside the market?
  • Can you leave this money invested for at least three to five years?
  • Have you cleared expensive debt such as credit card balances? Paying 25% interest while hoping for share gains rarely adds up.

Step 1: choose a stockbroker

All trading goes through a licensed stockbroker. Compare a few on:

  • Online platform: most offer the ATrad web and mobile platform or their own app.
  • Service: research reports, a contact person, branch access outside Colombo.
  • Payment options: how you send money for purchases and receive sale proceeds.

Brokerage on normal trades is set by the regulated fee structure, so price is rarely the deciding factor.

Step 2: open a CDS account

Download the official CSE Mobile App (Google Play or Apple App Store) and choose to open an account, or use your chosen broker's online form. You will typically need:

  1. Your National Identity Card
  2. Proof of address (for example a utility bill), if your address differs from your NIC
  3. Bank account details, such as a bank statement or the first page of your passbook
  4. A selfie taken in the app for identity verification
  5. Your mobile number and email, verified by one-time codes

After approval you receive your CDS account number and login details for your broker's trading platform. Non-residents follow a separate process and need an Inward Investment Account (IIA) at a bank. More detail: your CDS account explained.

Step 3: fund your trading account

Transfer money to your broker using the method they specify. Keep the receipt. Some brokers allow you to buy before funds arrive up to a limit; do not rely on credit you do not fully understand.

Step 4: learn the basics before buying

  • Costs: 1.12% on each buy and sell. See commission explained.
  • Break-even: the price must rise about 2.27% for you to break even. See break-even sell price.
  • Timing: trading runs 9:30 am to 2:30 pm on market days, and trades settle T+2. See market hours and settlement.
  • Company information: read quarterly results and announcements. See how to read quarterly results.

Step 5: place your first order

  1. Log in to the trading platform and search for the share symbol (for example COMB.N0000 for Commercial Bank voting shares).
  2. Look at the last traded price, the best buy and sell prices, and the quantity available at each.
  3. Choose Buy, enter quantity, choose a limit order and a price.
  4. Check the estimated total including fees before confirming.
  5. Watch the order status: it can fill completely, partly, or not at all if nobody sells at your price.

You will get a contract note after the trade, and the shares appear in your CDS account after settlement.

Step 6: track and review

Record the trade, including fees, in a tracker. Set a regular time, perhaps weekly, to review results, dividends and announcements rather than reacting to every price move. How to track your CSE shares describes a simple routine.

Alternatives to picking shares

If choosing individual companies is not for you, unit trusts licensed by the SEC invest across many shares or in fixed income on your behalf for a management fee. They are a different product with their own risks and costs; read the fund's documents before investing.

Protecting yourself

  • Only deal with licensed brokers and advisers; the SEC publishes lists.
  • Never share your trading password or one-time codes.
  • Be suspicious of guaranteed returns, urgent tips and "account management" offers from strangers.

Frequently asked questions

How much money do I need to start?

There is no official minimum for buying listed shares; you can buy a single share. Some brokers set their own minimums for certain services. Because fees are a percentage, small amounts are fine for learning, but trading tiny amounts very often is costly.

Can I open an account without visiting a broker?

Yes. Resident individuals can open a CDS account online through the CSE Mobile App or a broker’s web portal by submitting documents digitally.

Is investing in shares safe?

Shares can fall in value and companies can fail. The CSE and SEC regulate the market and brokers, which protects against many kinds of misconduct, but not against investment losses. Only invest money you can leave for several years.
