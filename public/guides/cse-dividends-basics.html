Dividends on the CSE: XD dates, payment and how much you really get

A dividend is a share of a company's profit paid to its shareholders, usually in cash. On the Colombo Stock Exchange (CSE), dividends follow a clear sequence of dates. Understanding them tells you whether you will receive a dividend, how much will actually reach your bank, and why the share price moves when it does.

Not investment advice. A company can change or skip its dividend at any time. Past dividends do not guarantee future ones.

The four dates that matter

DateWhat happens
AnnouncementThe board declares the dividend per share (DPS), usually with its quarterly or annual results, through a CSE announcement.
XD (ex-dividend) dateFrom this market day the share trades without the dividend. Buyers on or after this date do not receive it.
Record / entitlementThe company takes the list of shareholders entitled to the dividend from the CDS records.
Payment dateThe net dividend is credited to the bank account linked to your CDS account (or paid by other means stated in the notice).

Because CSE trades settle two market days after the trade (T+2), the XD date is set so that only buyers whose purchase settles in time are on the record. The simple rule for investors: own the share at the close of the day before the XD date.

How much actually reaches you

Cash dividends paid by Sri Lankan companies are subject to a 15% withholding tax, deducted by the company before payment. Since 1 January 2023 this has been treated as a final tax for dividends paid by resident companies, so most individual investors do not pay anything more on them. Some dividends are exempt (for example where the company's dividend is paid out of dividends it received itself), and the notice or dividend statement shows what was deducted.

Example: you own 2,000 shares and the company declares Rs 3.00 per share.

  • Gross dividend: 2,000 × 3.00 = Rs 6,000
  • Withholding tax at 15%: Rs 900
  • Net paid to you: Rs 5,100

For more detail see tax on dividends and share gains.

Why the price drops on the XD date

The day before the XD date, a buyer gets the share and the coming dividend. On the XD date they get only the share. So the price usually opens lower by about the dividend amount. If a Rs 100 share pays a Rs 5 dividend, a price near Rs 95 on the XD date is the market adjusting, not the company losing value. Your total wealth is roughly unchanged: a lower share price plus cash on the way.

This is why "buying just before the XD date to collect the dividend" rarely works as a quick profit: you pay the transaction cost twice and the price usually falls by roughly the dividend, while the dividend itself is taxed at 15%.

Dividend yield, two ways

  • Market yield = annual dividends per share ÷ current price. A Rs 4 total yearly dividend on a Rs 80 share is a 5% yield. Useful for comparing companies today.
  • Yield on your cost = annual dividends per share ÷ your average cost. If you bought at Rs 50, the same Rs 4 is an 8% yield on what you paid. Useful for judging how your own money is working.

A very high yield is worth a second look. It can mean the price has fallen because the market expects the dividend to be cut, or that last year's payout included a one-off special dividend.

Interim, final and special dividends

Many CSE companies pay an interim dividend during the financial year and a final dividend after the annual results, which usually needs shareholder approval at the AGM. A special dividend is a one-off payment, for example after selling an asset. When comparing yields, separate regular dividends from special ones.

Scrip dividends

Sometimes a company pays a dividend in new shares instead of cash (a scrip dividend), or offers a choice. You end up with more shares; the company keeps the cash. The circular explains the conversion price. Record the new shares in your portfolio so your quantity matches your CDS statement.

Practical checklist

  1. Make sure your CDS account has your correct bank details, or dividends may be delayed.
  2. Watch XD dates for shares you own or plan to buy (CSE Portfolio lists them for your holdings).
  3. On the payment date, check the amount against shares held × DPS × 0.85.
  4. Keep the dividend statements; they show the tax deducted.
  5. Record dividends with your holdings so your total return includes them.

Frequently asked questions

If I buy a share on the XD date, do I get the dividend?

No. Shares bought on or after the XD date trade without the right to the announced dividend. To receive it you must buy before the XD date, so that the purchase settles in time for you to be on the shareholder register.

If I sell on the XD date, do I still get the dividend?

Yes. If you owned the shares at the close of the market day before the XD date, you keep the right to the dividend even if you sell on or after the XD date.

Why did my share price fall on the XD date?

From the XD date the share no longer carries the dividend, so buyers pay less for it. A fall roughly equal to the dividend per share is normal and is not a loss of value: the cash will reach you on the payment date.
