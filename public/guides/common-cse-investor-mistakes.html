12 common mistakes new CSE investors make (and how to avoid them)

Most early losses on the Colombo Stock Exchange (CSE) do not come from bad luck. They come from a short list of avoidable habits. Here are the ones we see most often, with a plain fix for each.

Not investment advice. This page explains common pitfalls. It does not recommend buying or selling any share.

1. Forgetting the 1.12% on each side

A share has to rise about 2.27% before you break even after buying and selling costs. Selling at a "small profit" is often a loss. Fix: know your break-even sell price before you sell.

2. Trading too often

Ten round trips a year cost about 22% of the money traded in fees alone. Frequent trading needs consistently large gains just to stand still. Fix: trade less, and write down why before each trade.

3. Acting on tips from groups and social media

Messages that a little-known share is "about to fly" are a classic setup for pump-and-dump schemes, where early holders sell to those who arrive late. The SEC has acted against people behind abnormal price movements, but losses by late buyers are rarely recovered. Fix: read the company's own announcements and results; ignore urgency.

4. Buying just before the XD date for the dividend

The price usually drops by about the dividend on the XD date, the dividend is taxed 15%, and you pay fees twice. Fix: understand how dividends and XD dates work before trying this.

5. Watching the price, not the business

Share prices follow profits and dividends over time. Many investors never open the quarterly results of companies they own. Fix: spend ten minutes on each company's results each quarter; see how to read quarterly results.

6. Ignoring liquidity

Some CSE shares trade only a few thousand rupees a day. Buying is easy; selling a meaningful amount without pushing the price down can take days. Fix: check average daily turnover and the order book depth before buying.

7. Putting everything in one share or one sector

A single company can have a bad year, a rights issue, or a scandal. Fix: spread money across several companies and sectors. There is no perfect number, but one share is rarely enough.

8. Averaging down without a reason

Buying more of a falling share lowers your average cost, but if the business is getting worse it also increases the loss. Fix: only add if your original reason for owning it still holds; see average cost: what happens when you buy more.

9. Using money you will need soon

Share prices can stay low for years. Money for school fees, a wedding or an emergency next year should not be in shares. Fix: keep an emergency fund and short-term savings outside the market.

10. Misreading a cheap price

A Rs 5 share is not cheaper than a Rs 500 share. What matters is the price relative to profits and assets. Fix: learn P/E and P/BV; compare companies, not price tags.

11. Not recording rights, bonus and splits

After a bonus issue your quantity rises and the price falls. If you do not update your records, your profit and loss look wildly wrong. Fix: update quantity and cost the day the corporate action happens.

12. Letting a broker or "adviser" trade for you without authority

Only licensed investment advisers and managers may give personal advice or manage money. Unauthorised trading in your account is a serious problem. Fix: read every contract note; report trades you did not place.

A simple habit that prevents most of these

Before every buy, write three lines: why you are buying, what price you are paying including fees, and what would make you sell. Before every sell, re-read those lines. This takes two minutes and removes most impulse decisions.

Frequently asked questions

Is it a mistake to invest a small amount?

No. Starting small while you learn how trading, fees and settlement work is sensible. Just remember that the 1.12% fee applies whatever the size, so very frequent small trades lose a lot to costs.

How do I know if a tip is a pump-and-dump?

Warning signs include pressure to buy immediately, promises of guaranteed or very high returns, little-known shares with low turnover, and messages from people you do not know. The SEC has taken action against abnormal price movements; when in doubt, stay out.
