Tax on dividends and share gains in Sri Lanka

For most individual investors on the Colombo Stock Exchange, tax is simpler than it first looks. Cash dividends have tax deducted before they reach you, profits from selling listed shares are outside capital gains tax, and a small levy is built into every trade. This guide explains each part, with the caveat that tax law in Sri Lanka has changed several times recently.

Not tax or investment advice. This is a general explanation for resident individual investors. Your position can differ. Confirm with the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) or a qualified tax adviser.

1. Dividends: 15% withheld at source

When a Sri Lankan company pays a cash dividend, it deducts withholding tax (WHT) at 15% and pays the rest to shareholders. Since 1 January 2023, this deduction on dividends paid by resident companies has been treated as a final withholding payment. In practice that means most individual investors do not pay any further tax on those dividends, and do not get a refund of the 15% either. An IRD circular issued in June 2026 again lists dividend payments at 15%.

Some dividends are exempt from WHT, for example where a company passes on dividends it received from another resident company, or dividends from certain BOI-approved businesses. The company's dividend notice or statement shows the gross amount, any tax deducted and the net payment; keep it as your record.

Non-resident investors

Dividends paid to non-resident shareholders are also generally subject to 15% at source, which may be reduced under a double tax treaty between Sri Lanka and the investor's country of residence. A withholding certificate from the company is used as proof when the money is sent abroad. Non-residents should take advice in both countries.

2. Selling shares: listed shares are outside capital gains tax

Sri Lanka reintroduced capital gains tax (CGT) from 1 April 2018 on gains from realising "investment assets". However, quoted shares listed on the Colombo Stock Exchange are excluded. The Inland Revenue (Amendment) Act No. 11 of 2026, certified in June 2026, revised CGT rates for other assets, but commentary on the new law notes that listed CSE shares remain excluded.

So if you buy shares at Rs 50 and sell at Rs 80, there is no CGT on the Rs 30 gain. Losses are likewise not deductible against other income. This exclusion is one reason the transaction cost and dividend tax are the main costs for CSE investors.

Unlisted shares, shares in private companies, land and other investment assets are treated differently. Buying shares through a company or as a business of share dealing can also change the treatment; that is a question for a tax adviser.

3. The share transaction levy

Every buy and every sell on the CSE pays a share transaction levy of 0.30% of the trade value. It is part of the standard 1.12% transaction cost, alongside brokerage and the CSE, CDS and SEC fees. You do not pay it separately; your broker collects it. See commission explained.

4. Other income from shares

  • Scrip dividends (new shares instead of cash): follow the company circular, which states the tax treatment.
  • Rights you sell during the renunciation period: proceeds from trading listed rights are generally treated like trading listed securities, but confirm with a tax adviser if the amounts are large.
  • Interest on cash left with your broker or in a savings account is taxed differently: advance income tax on interest was raised to 10% from 1 April 2025.

Keeping records

  1. Keep dividend statements showing gross dividend, WHT and net amount.
  2. Keep contract notes for every trade, even though gains on listed shares are not taxed; they prove cost and dates.
  3. Keep CDS statements for corporate actions.

Good records make it easy to answer any question from the IRD, a bank or an adviser, and they make your own profit-and-loss figures reliable.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to declare dividends in my income tax return?

Since 1 January 2023, dividends paid by resident companies have been subject to a 15% withholding tax that is treated as a final tax, so they are generally not taxed again. If you file a return for other income, ask a tax adviser how to show dividend income and the tax already deducted.

Do I pay capital gains tax when I sell CSE shares at a profit?

Gains on quoted shares listed on the Colombo Stock Exchange are excluded from capital gains tax under current Inland Revenue rules. This was confirmed again in commentary on the Inland Revenue (Amendment) Act No. 11 of 2026. Rules for unlisted shares and other assets are different.

Is the share transaction levy a tax?

Yes, it is a government levy of 0.30% of trade value, charged to both buyer and seller as part of the 1.12% transaction cost.
