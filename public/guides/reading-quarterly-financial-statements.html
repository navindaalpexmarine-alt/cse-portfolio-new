How to read a CSE company’s quarterly results in 15 minutes

Every listed company on the Colombo Stock Exchange (CSE) publishes interim financial statements each quarter. They are the most direct evidence of how the business is doing, yet many investors never open them. You do not need to be an accountant: fifteen minutes and a few simple checks tell you most of what matters.

Not investment advice. This guide explains how to read the statements, not what to conclude about any company.

Where to find them

Results appear as financial announcements on the CSE website, usually as a PDF. Company websites often carry them too. CSE Portfolio's News tab lists new filings for the shares you own.

Minute 1–3: the summary and the period

  • Check which period it covers: the quarter (three months) and the cumulative figures for the year to date.
  • Note the comparison period, usually the same quarter last year. Sri Lankan businesses are often seasonal (tourism, plantations, retail at festival times), so comparing with the previous quarter can mislead.
  • Check if the figures are group (consolidated, including subsidiaries) or company only. For diversified groups, the group figures matter most.

Minute 3–7: the income statement

LineWhat to ask
RevenueGrowing or shrinking compared with last year? By how much?
Gross profit and marginGross profit ÷ revenue. Is the company keeping its pricing power?
Operating expensesRising faster than revenue?
Finance costsInterest on borrowings. Large and rising finance costs eat profit.
Other income / one-off itemsGains from selling assets or currency movements that will not repeat.
Profit attributable to equity holdersThe profit that belongs to the company's shareholders, after minority interests.
Earnings per share (EPS)Profit per share. Add the last four quarters for a yearly figure.

Quick check: if revenue rose 10% but profit rose 60%, look for a one-off gain or a fall in finance costs that explains it.

Minute 7–11: the balance sheet

  • Total equity and net assets per share: used for the P/BV ratio (P/E and P/BV explained).
  • Borrowings (interest-bearing loans): compare with equity. Debt much larger than equity means more risk when rates rise or sales fall.
  • Cash and short-term investments: a buffer for bad times and a source of dividends.
  • Receivables and inventory: if these grow much faster than revenue, customers may be paying slowly or stock may be piling up.

Minute 11–13: the cash flow statement

Profit is an accounting figure; cash is what pays dividends and debts. Look at net cash from operating activities. Over a year it should broadly track profit. If profit is high but operating cash flow is consistently low or negative, find out why.

Also check investing activities (spending on new plant or acquisitions) and financing (new loans, repayments, dividends paid).

Minute 13–15: the notes and share information

  • Top 20 shareholders and public holding: who owns the company and how much is freely traded.
  • Directors' shareholdings: changes can be informative.
  • Events after the reporting date: dividends declared, acquisitions, legal cases.
  • Segment information for groups: which businesses drive the profit.

A simple scorecard

  1. Revenue growth vs last year: up / flat / down
  2. Profit attributable growth vs last year: up / flat / down
  3. Any large one-off item? yes / no
  4. Debt vs equity: lower / similar / higher than a year ago
  5. Operating cash flow positive? yes / no

Write the five answers in your notes each quarter. After a year you will have a clear picture of the trend, which is worth more than any single quarter.

Annual reports

Once a year the audited financial statements come with the annual report, which adds the chairman's and CEO's reviews, risk discussion and the auditor's opinion. Read the auditor's report: a "qualified" opinion or an "emphasis of matter" deserves attention.

Frequently asked questions

When are quarterly results published?

Listed companies must publish interim financial statements within a set time after each quarter ends, under CSE listing rules. Many publish near the deadline, so results for a quarter often appear several weeks after it ends.

Are interim statements audited?

Usually not. Quarterly interim statements are prepared by management and are normally unaudited. The annual financial statements in the annual report are audited.

Where can I see results for shares I own?

They are published as financial announcements on the CSE website. CSE Portfolio’s News tab highlights new financial announcements for companies you hold.
