CSE buy & sell commission explained

On the Colombo Stock Exchange you rarely receive (or pay) exactly the headline share price. Brokerage and related charges sit on top of the trade. Understanding them avoids false “profit” on paper.

Not investment advice. Fee schedules change; always check your broker’s current rates and contract notes.

What “commission” usually means in practice

Retail investors often summarise all percentage-based trading costs as a single commission rate (for example around 1% depending on broker and package). The exact split may include brokerage, exchange-related fees and taxes. For personal tracking, many people use one combined percentage on both buy and sell, then refine later from real contract notes.

Buy side: you pay more than price × quantity

Effective cash out ≈ (price × quantity) × (1 + buy cost rate), or price × quantity plus fixed fees if your broker uses a different structure.

Example (illustrative only): 100 shares at LKR 50 with 1.12% total buy costs → share value LKR 5,000, costs about LKR 56, total out about LKR 5,056. Your true cost per share is higher than LKR 50.

Sell side: you receive less than price × quantity

Effective cash in ≈ (price × quantity) × (1 − sell cost rate). The market may show a gain versus your purchase price while you still lose money after both sides’ costs.

Why this matters for “am I up?”

Mark-to-market profit using last traded price ignores costs already paid on the way in and costs you will pay on the way out. A honest personal view uses:

  • Average cost including buy charges
  • Estimated sell charges at a realistic rate
  • Optional: dividends received while you held

Practical tips

  • Read one full contract note and list every line item once; map them into your tracker’s commission field.
  • If you use more than one broker, either track rates per broker or use a conservative higher rate.
  • Recheck rates after promotions end — introductory brokerage can expire.
