How to track your CSE shares properly

Knowing what you own, what it cost and what it is worth today sounds simple. In practice, fees, dividends, partial sales, rights issues and accounts with more than one broker make it easy to lose track. A good record answers one question honestly: if I sold everything today, how much better or worse off would I be?

Not investment advice. This guide is about record-keeping, not about which shares to hold.

What to record for every holding

FieldWhy it matters
Symbol (e.g. COMB.N0000)Voting (N) and non-voting (X) shares of the same company are different securities with different prices.
QuantityChanges with buys, sells, bonus issues, rights and splits.
Total cash spent, including feesThe true cost. Your average cost per share comes from this.
Purchase datesUseful for judging how long money has been invested and for matching dividends.
Dividends received (net of tax)Part of your return. Ignoring them understates income shares.
Broker / CDS accountIf you use more than one broker, so you can reconcile with each statement.

Three numbers to look at, in this order

  1. Market value = quantity × last traded price. What the holding is worth on paper.
  2. Net sale value = market value × (1 − 1.12%). What you would actually receive after the selling fee. This is the honest "worth today" figure.
  3. Unrealised profit or loss = net sale value − total cash spent. Positive means selling today would make money after all fees.

Many apps show market value minus purchase price, which overstates profit by about 2.27% of the position. On a Rs 500,000 portfolio that is more than Rs 11,000 of profit that does not really exist.

Realised versus unrealised

Unrealised profit or loss is on shares you still own; it changes every day. Realised profit or loss is locked in when you sell. Keep the two apart. If you sell your winners and keep your losers, your realised figure can look excellent while the remaining portfolio quietly shrinks. A full picture is realised + unrealised + dividends received.

Measuring against the market

A 10% gain in a year sounds good, but if the All Share Price Index (ASPI) rose 25% over the same period, a simple index-tracking approach would have done better. Comparing your portfolio's change with the ASPI over the same dates is a useful reality check. It is not a race, and different goals (income, safety, growth) justify different results, but the comparison stops you from judging performance in a vacuum.

A simple weekly routine (15 minutes)

  1. Reconcile. Make sure the quantities match your CDS or broker statement. Corporate actions are the usual cause of differences.
  2. Check upcoming dates. XD dates for dividends, rights trading periods and AGM dates for companies you own.
  3. Read the announcements. Quarterly results, director dealings and other CSE announcements for your holdings. You do not need to read every page; the summary and the income statement are a good start.
  4. Note your reasons. One line per holding: why you own it and what would make you reconsider. Re-reading this when prices swing helps avoid emotional decisions.

Corporate actions that change your record

  • Bonus issue or split: more shares, same total cost, lower cost per share. See bonus issues and share splits.
  • Rights issue: new shares bought at the rights price; add the cash you paid to your total cost. See rights issues.
  • Scrip dividend: shares instead of cash; record the new shares at the value stated in the company circular.
  • Name or symbol change: same holding, new label. Keep the history attached.

Keeping records safe

Keep contract notes, dividend statements and CDS statements, either on paper or as PDFs in one folder. They are your evidence if there is ever a dispute and they make tax questions easy to answer. If you keep a spreadsheet, back it up; if you use an app, make sure your data is tied to an account and not only to one phone.

Frequently asked questions

My broker already shows my portfolio. Why track it myself?

Broker platforms usually show market value and an average price, but often ignore fees, dividends received and shares held with other brokers. Your own record joins everything up and keeps history after you sell.

How often should I update my record?

Record each trade, dividend and corporate action the day it happens. Check the overall picture weekly. Daily checking adds little for long-term investors and can encourage rushed decisions.

Can I import my holdings instead of typing them?

Yes. CSE Portfolio can read the portfolio Excel file that the ATrad trading platform exports, so you can upload it instead of entering each share by hand.
