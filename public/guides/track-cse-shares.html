How to track your CSE shares

If you buy and sell shares on the Colombo Stock Exchange (CSE), a clear personal record helps you see cost, profit or loss, and dividend income — without relying only on memory or scattered broker statements.

Not investment advice. This guide is about record-keeping only. Always verify trades with your broker and the official CSE.

Why tracking matters

Broker apps show recent activity, but over months or years you may hold several stocks, buy the same counter more than once, and receive dividends. Without your own log it is hard to answer simple questions: What did I actually pay on average? How much have I received in dividends this year? Which positions are up or down after costs?

A portfolio tracker does not replace professional advice. It organises your data so you can review it calmly.

What to record for every trade

  • Symbol — the CSE ticker (for example SAMP.N0000 style codes used in market data).
  • Date — trade date as on the contract note.
  • Side — buy or sell.
  • Quantity — number of shares.
  • Price — price per share.
  • Costs — brokerage and other charges, or a commission percentage you apply consistently.

From quantity, price and costs you can compute total cash out (for buys) or net cash in (for sells). Keeping costs in the same place as the trade avoids understating what you need to recover when you sell.

Average cost when you buy more than once

Many investors buy the same share at different prices. A common approach is a weighted average cost: total amount spent (including costs) divided by total shares still held. When you sell part of a holding, reduce quantity and keep the same average cost for the remainder unless you use a different accounting method required for tax or reporting.

Whatever method you choose, write it down and use it consistently so your history stays comparable.

A simple weekly routine

  1. After any trade, enter it the same day while the contract note is fresh.
  2. Once a week, update market prices (manually or with a tool that loads live CSE-related data).
  3. Once a month, check dividends announced or paid and log them against the right symbol.
  4. Export or back up your data occasionally so a phone change or browser clear does not wipe your history.
