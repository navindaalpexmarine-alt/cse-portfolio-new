Market halts and circuit breakers on the CSE

Circuit breakers are automatic pauses in trading when prices fall very fast. They give investors time to take in news and stop panic selling feeding on itself. The Colombo Stock Exchange (CSE) uses a three-step circuit breaker linked to the S&P SL20 index, and it can also halt trading in individual shares.

Not investment advice. Thresholds come from directives by the Securities and Exchange Commission of Sri Lanka (SEC) and can change. Check the latest CSE notices.

The three-tier market-wide circuit breaker

Introduced by an SEC directive in 2020 and kept in place since, the structure is based on falls in the S&P SL20 compared with the previous day's close:

TriggerFall from previous closeWhat happens
First circuit breaker5%All trading halted for 30 minutes
Second circuit breakerA further 2.5% after trading resumes (7.5% in total)Trading halted for another 30 minutes
Third circuit breakerA further 2.5% after trading resumes (10% in total)Trading halted and the market closed for the day

The S&P SL20 is used because its 20 large, liquid companies are hard to move with small trades, so a big fall in it reflects genuine market-wide selling. See ASPI vs S&P SL20.

Real examples

  • March 2020: 30-minute halts were imposed on several days as the S&P SL20 fell more than 5% amid the start of the pandemic.
  • May 2020: on reopening after 51 days, the index breached the 10% threshold within seconds and trading closed for the day.
  • April 2025: regular trading was halted when a fall of more than 5% triggered the circuit breaker during global market turmoil.

Halts on individual shares

Separately from market-wide halts, the CSE can halt trading in a single security, for example:

  • Before a company releases price-sensitive information, so everyone gets it at the same time.
  • To get a clarification from a company about a rumour or press report.
  • When the price or volume moves unusually; the trading system has preset limits that can halt a share automatically.

Longer suspensions can be imposed when a company breaks listing rules, for example by failing to publish financial statements on time. In January 2026 the CSE halted trading after abnormal price movements and the SEC later said it had identified those responsible.

What a halt means for you

  1. No trades happen during the halt. You cannot sell during it, which is the point.
  2. Check your open orders afterwards. Prices may reopen far from where you placed them.
  3. Read the news that caused the move before reacting.
  4. Avoid market orders right after a halt; use limit orders so you control the price.

Why circuit breakers exist

In a small market, a wave of sell orders can push prices far below what most investors believe is fair, simply because buyers step back. A pause lets buyers return, gives companies time to issue clarifications and lets investors check facts. Critics argue halts can delay price discovery and that investors who need to sell are trapped. Most markets, including India and the United States, use similar mechanisms.

Frequently asked questions

Do circuit breakers apply when the market rises?

The market-wide circuit breakers described here are triggered by falls in the S&P SL20 index. Individual shares can still be halted for unusual price rises, pending announcements, or market surveillance reasons.

What happens to my orders during a halt?

During a market halt no trades are executed. What happens to orders already in the system depends on the CSE’s rules at the time; check your order book on your trading platform after the halt and re-enter orders if needed.

Has the CSE ever closed for the day because of a 10% fall?

Yes. In May 2020, when the market reopened after a long COVID-19 closure, the S&P SL20 fell more than 10% in under a minute and trading ended for the day.
